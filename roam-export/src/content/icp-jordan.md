# Jordan — Roam's Ideal Customer Profile

> This document serves two purposes:
> 1. **Video ad script material** — narrate Jordan's day to show we understand the problem
> 2. **Website copy** — the "we get it" section that makes visitors feel seen

---

## The basics

**Name:** Jordan
**Age:** 29
**Location:** Capitol Hill, Seattle (but could be any mid-density urban neighborhood)
**Job:** Product designer at a mid-size SaaS company, hybrid — 2 days in office, 3 remote
**Living situation:** Studio apartment with a cat named Soup
**Relationship:** Single, not really looking, too tired to care right now
**Income:** $78K — comfortable enough that money isn't the stressor, time and energy are

---

## The psychographics (what matters more than demographics)

- Jordan is not depressed. Jordan is **flat**. There's a difference. Depression is a clinical weight. Flatness is what happens when every day looks the same and nothing breaks the pattern.
- Jordan knows the "right" things to do: go outside, see friends, cook real food, move their body. They've read the articles. They've tried the apps. The knowing isn't the problem. **The doing is the problem**, because doing requires a reason, and right now no reason feels strong enough to overcome the inertia of the couch.
- Jordan's social life didn't collapse — it **evaporated**. Nobody had a fight. Nobody moved away. Everyone just got busy, and "let's hang out soon" became the lie they all tell each other every two weeks over text.
- Jordan has **notification fatigue**. Every app wants attention. Slack, email, Instagram, the fitness app they downloaded in January that still sends push notifications about "crushing their goals." Everything is loud. Jordan craves something quiet.
- Jordan doesn't want to be *coached*. They've been coached. By apps, by podcasts, by well-meaning friends. "You should try running." "Have you tried meditation?" "You just need to get out more." They know. **They need a reason, not a lecture.**

## A Tuesday in Jordan's life

### 7:14 AM — The alarm

The alarm goes off. Jordan hits snooze. Not because they're tired — they slept fine — but because there's nothing pulling them out of bed. No one is expecting them anywhere until the 9:30 standup. They scroll Instagram for 11 minutes. They don't enjoy it. They just do it because the phone is there and their brain isn't awake enough to choose something else.

### 7:48 AM — The apartment

Jordan makes coffee. The French press they bought when they moved in, when the apartment still felt like a fresh start. They eat a granola bar standing at the counter, looking out the window at the street below. Someone is walking a golden retriever. Jordan thinks, *I should go outside today.* They think this every day. It doesn't happen most days.

### 8:15 AM — The desk

The desk is four feet from the bed. The commute is standing up. Jordan opens Slack, Figma, Notion. The cat jumps on the keyboard. This is the most physical contact Jordan will have with another living thing until... actually, this might be it for the day.

### 9:30 AM — The standup

Camera on. "Hey everyone." Fifteen minutes of status updates. Someone makes a joke. Jordan laughs. The call ends and the silence comes back. The apartment is very quiet. Jordan puts on a podcast — not because they want to listen, but because the silence is worse.

### 12:20 PM — Lunch

Jordan UberEats a salad. $18 for something they could make for $4 but won't, because making food requires wanting to eat, and eating has become mechanical. They eat at the desk. They don't taste it. They look at the door. *I should go for a walk.* They open Twitter instead.

### 2:00 PM — The afternoon wall

The work is fine. Jordan is good at their job. But there's a specific kind of emptiness that comes from being good at something you do alone in a room. The Figma file is clean. The Slack messages are answered. The afternoon stretches. Jordan's Fitbit says 287 steps. The Fitbit doesn't help. It just makes them feel guilty. Guilt doesn't make you move. It makes you sit still and feel bad about sitting still.

### 4:45 PM — The window

The light outside is golden. Jordan can see it hitting the buildings across the street. It's the kind of light that makes you want to be outside. Jordan thinks, *I should go for a walk before it gets dark.* They open their laptop to finish one more thing. The light is gone by the time they look up.

### 6:30 PM — The evening

Jordan heats up leftover pasta. They sit on the couch. They open Netflix. They scroll for 7 minutes, pick something, watch 20 minutes, pick something else. The cat is on their lap. Jordan texts their friend Mika: *"we should hang out this week."* Mika says *"yes definitely!! maybe thursday?"* Jordan says *"perfect."* Thursday will come and one of them will cancel. They both know this. They send the texts anyway because the ritual of planning is its own small comfort.

### 8:15 PM — The phone

Jordan opens Instagram again. Then TikTok. Then back to Instagram. They see a friend's story — a group dinner, everyone laughing, a restaurant with warm lighting. Jordan feels something but can't name it. It's not jealousy exactly. It's more like watching a movie of a life that used to be theirs. They double-tap a heart. They put the phone down. They pick it up again.

### 9:40 PM — The thought

Jordan is in bed. Soup is curled up by their feet. The apartment is quiet. Jordan has the thought they have every night: *Today was fine. Nothing was wrong. So why does it feel like nothing happened?*

They set the alarm for 7:14. They'll hit snooze. They know it already.

### 10:02 PM — The thing that doesn't exist yet

Here's what Jordan doesn't know: tomorrow, if something on their phone said "hey, let's go" in a voice that wasn't selling anything, wasn't coaching anything, wasn't tracking anything — just a warm presence that wanted to walk with them for fifteen minutes — they'd put their shoes on. They would. The activation energy isn't that high. It's not a mountain. It's a doorknob. Someone just has to give them a reason to turn it.

**That's Roam. That's Echo. That's the product.**

## The emotional arc (for the video ad)

The video should follow this structure:

**0:00–0:30 — "The Loop"**
Quick cuts of the daily loop. Phone alarm. Scroll. Desk. Slack. UberEats. Couch. Netflix scroll. All shot in flat, desaturated color. No music — just ambient apartment sounds. The viewer should feel the quiet monotony.

*Voiceover (Jordan's inner voice, not a narrator):*
> "I keep telling myself I'll go outside today. I say it every morning. And then it's 9 PM and I didn't."

**0:30–0:45 — "The Window"**
Jordan looking out the window. The golden light. The person walking the dog. The moment of wanting to go but not having a reason.

*Voiceover:*
> "It's not that I can't. It's that nobody's asking me to. Every app wants me to work out. Nobody just wants to walk with me."

**0:45–1:00 — "The Turn"**
Phone buzzes. It's not a notification. It's Echo. A warm golden glow on the screen. A soft voice: *"Hey. It's nice out. Want to go?"*

Color shifts. Warmer. Jordan puts shoes on. The door opens.

**1:00–1:20 — "The Walk"**
Jordan walking. Not exercising — walking. AirPods in. Echo's blob on the phone screen, pulsing gently. Echo asks: *"What's the best thing you ate today?"* Jordan laughs. Actually laughs. The streets are beautiful in that way you only notice when you're actually on them.

The map on the phone shows fog clearing. A golden trail behind them.

**1:20–1:30 — "The After"**
Jordan back home. Same apartment. But something is different. The heat map on their phone shows one glowing route through their neighborhood. It's small. It's theirs.

*Text on screen:*
> **Roam.**
> **A friend to walk with when no one else is around.**

**1:30 — End card**
Echo blob breathing. URL.

## Key quotes for interview-style content

If you're filming "interview" segments (even if they're acted), these are the things Jordan would say:

1. *"I haven't had a real reason to go outside in like... weeks? Unless you count the grocery store."*
2. *"I tried Strava. I tried Peloton. They all want me to be someone I'm not right now."*
3. *"I don't need a coach. I need someone to just... walk with me. That's it."*
4. *"The weirdest part is I'm not sad. I'm just... nothing. Every day is fine. That's the problem."*
5. *"I know walking would help. I've read the studies. Knowing doesn't make you put your shoes on."*
6. *"I FaceTime my mom every Sunday. That's my most meaningful human interaction most weeks. I'm 29."*
7. *"I have 400 friends on Instagram and I haven't seen any of them in a month."*
8. *"If something just said 'hey, let's go' — not 'time to crush your workout' — just 'let's go' — I think I would."*

## For the website — the "We get it" section

This should be a short, punchy section on the landing page. Not the full narrative — just enough to make visitors feel seen.

**Headline:** "Sound familiar?"

**Cards (3-4 short vignettes):**

- "You told yourself you'd go outside today. It's 8 PM. You didn't."
- "Your Fitbit says 300 steps. It doesn't make you move. It just makes you feel guilty."
- "You haven't seen a friend in person in three weeks. Not because anything happened. Everyone just got busy."
- "You're not sad. You're not broken. You're just flat. And nobody's giving you a reason to put your shoes on."

**Closing line:** "Roam isn't here to fix you. Echo's just here to walk with you."
